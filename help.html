Answering a question

Enter the derivative, then press Enter or select Check answer. Equivalent expressions are accepted; you do not need to simplify. Use radians for trigonometric functions.

  • Your result appears inside the answer box.
  • ✓ Correct: select Next question or press Enter again, or let the short countdown along the bottom of the box finish. With the math keyboard open, the blue key at the bottom right of the keyboard changes from Check to Next.
  • ! Not quite: edit your answer and try again. The input keeps its focus.
  • Check your input: the line under the answer box says what to fix, such as a missing bracket, or asks you to try another equivalent form. This does not count as a wrong answer.
Typing formulas

Select Math keyboard on a phone or a computer. It is one page, laid out like a calculator: sin, cos, tan, sec, csc, and cot on the left; the numbers in the middle; fraction, ·, −, and + in one column; and your question’s variable, powers, square roots, and delete on the right. Empty boxes show where you type next. The arrow keys are at the bottom left, and the blue Check key at the bottom right checks your answer, then becomes Next.

Many keys have a second symbol. On a computer, it is shown faintly in the key’s top-right corner. Hold a key to see it in a bubble, then let go to type it (slide off the key to cancel). Or tap ⇧: the keys that change turn blue, and the next key you press types its second symbol. Tap ⇧ twice to keep it on; tap it again to turn it off. Second symbols: sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹, tan⁻¹ (they type arcsin, arccos, arctan); sec², csc², cot²; ÷ on the fraction key; your variable’s power and square root on the power and root keys; absolute value on (; the constant e on e▫; log with a base on ln; the letters x y z, u v w, r s t on 7–9, 4–6, 1–3; θ on 0; and π on the decimal point.

Questions about functions f and g change four keys: tan, sec, csc and cot become f(▫), f′(▫), g(▫) and g′(▫). Each one types the function with an empty box for its inside, for example f′(x³). On a computer you can also type f'(x^3).

Hint? and Skip are at the top left of the keyboard, and the button at the top right hides it; select Math keyboard to bring it back.

The math keyboard follows the same editing rules as typing. Use ^ for an exponent, / for a fraction, and sqrt for a square root. Type sin, cos, or ln for functions.

Use the arrow keys to move out of an exponent or fraction. Parentheses group terms. The variable shown in the question may be x, y, t, or θ. Vector questions have one answer box per component.

Hints and retries

Need a hint? reveals the rule, then the structure, then the worked solution. Hints are there to help you learn; they mark that question as assisted. Repeating a submission on the same question does not add another review or another practiced question.

Follow your learning path

Open Progress, then select a level to see its skills and review dates in one place. For each skill you have started, the path shows how likely you are to use it correctly, as a percentage.

Learning
Every first-try answer updates the skills the question uses. A correct answer raises all of them; a wrong answer lowers them, most of all the weakest one. Hints and retries count as not correct.
Basic ✓
At 80% a skill passes and the skills that build on it open. An early skill (levels 1–2) passes after one correct first try; later skills need two.
Mixed
About one question in three combines a skill with skills you have already passed.
Ready
At 85%. Spaced reviews help you keep the skill; a correct mixed question also counts as a review of the simpler skills in it. Ready does not mean you never need to revisit it.
Rebuilding
If a passed skill drops below 60%, it gets a few focused questions, with other questions in between, until it is back at 80%.

The levels go from single rules to combinations: 1 constants, powers, sums and roots; 2 eˣ, ln, sin and cos; 3 the product and quotient rules, tan, cot, sec and csc, then Combinations I (two rules, no chain rule); 4 the chain rule and Combinations II (the chain rule with one other rule); 5 implicit and inverse-function derivatives, arcsin, arccos and arctan, higher derivatives, Combinations III (harder combinations) and abstract functions (derivatives with f and g). Your teacher can turn off skills; they show as Not included.

The next level unlocks when every enabled skill in the current level has passed. Unlocked levels stay open. When everything available is waiting for a review, you’ll see All caught up.

Streaks and daily practice

Your streak counts first-try, unassisted correct answers. A wrong answer, teaching hint, or unanswered skip resets it. Retries do not extend the streak. Celebrations begin at five in a row; at ten and above, each success gets a full-screen celebration. Your device’s reduced-motion setting is respected.

Today’s count increases once per question at its first valid check or teaching hint. It follows your device’s local date; retrying and skipping do not add to it.

Updates and version

After the website is updated, a What’s new window appears the next time you open practice on a device that already has your progress. It lists every change since you last looked and appears only once. Reopen it any time from What’s new at the bottom of the page, which also shows the current version number.

Pick up on another device

  1. On this device, open Move progress → Export progress.
  2. Scan the QR with the other device’s camera. A new device opens the website, restores the snapshot, and starts practice. If it already has saved progress, confirm before replacing it.
  3. Or copy the progress code and use Import progress. You can also choose a saved QR image.

If you see This progress code can’t be read, copy the whole code again, or export a new one on the other device. If you are asked to reload, refresh this page to get the latest version, then try again.

Each export is a snapshot. Export again after practicing to bring the new progress across. There is no automatic sync and the two devices’ histories are not merged.

What is saved?

Your browser keeps your current question and draft, skill progress, review schedule, streak, and daily counts. The portable snapshot keeps learning state and short question identifiers, not question formulas or your draft. It includes no name or account.

Progress is not uploaded to a student database. Clearing browser data or using a temporary/private session can lose local progress. Keep an export if you need a backup.

On iPhone: Safari can delete a website’s saved data after about a week of using Safari without opening that website. Add practice to your Home Screen (Share → Add to Home Screen) and open it from there, and export a code before a long break. Practice also asks your browser to keep its data.

If practice says it couldn’t open your saved progress, select Reload; your progress has not been changed. Continue without saving lets you practice, but nothing is kept unless you export it. Anyone with a code or QR can read that snapshot, so share it only as intended.

Restore or start over

Restore backup brings back the local state saved before the last replacement. Reset progress starts over after confirmation and retains a local recovery backup.

If scanning is difficult, use Save QR image or copy the code. Camera scanning needs HTTPS or localhost; a plain local-network HTTP address may only support code or image import.
